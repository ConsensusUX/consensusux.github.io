# Glossary

Alphabetical definitions of domain-specific terms used across the ConsensusUX wiki.

---

**Affinity Group**
: A small, self-organized group of people who share common goals, values, or interests and act collectively. In ConsensusUX, affinity groups are the primary unit of organization. The term originates in anarchist organizing tradition and was central to Occupy movement structure through its spokes council model.

**Anarchist Cybernetics**
: A theoretical framework developed by Tom Swann (2021) that applies cybernetic systems theory — the study of communication, control, and feedback in complex systems — to anarchist and horizontal political organizing. Anarchist cybernetics provides design principles for radical platforms: reversibility, flexibility, requisite variety, and mechanisms that reinforce collective self-organization without centralizing control.

**Chain Messages**
: A communication modality in ConsensusUX in which a message is passed along a defined chain of recipients rather than broadcast to all members simultaneously. Chain messages are used for communications requiring sequential acknowledgment or graduated disclosure, and preserve privacy by limiting each recipient's view to their own position in the chain.

**Civic Tech**
: A movement and practice using digital technology to increase public participation in civic processes — reporting issues, accessing government services, providing feedback on local decisions. Andrew Schrock (2018) defines it as using technology "as a way to get more people involved in figuring out what the solution should be." ConsensusUX critiques civic tech for its technocratic tendencies and disconnection from genuine political power, while building on its participatory impulse.

**Consensus Decision-Making (CDM)**
: A decision-making process in which a group works toward proposals that all members can support, rather than imposing the will of a majority on a minority. CDM includes the right to block proposals that a member finds seriously harmful, and when blocked, invites proposers to modify the proposal to achieve genuine consensus. Distinguished from majoritarian democracy by its structural orientation toward equitable outcomes. See also: [Academic Foundation — Consensus Decision-Making](academic-foundation#consensus-decision-making-cdm).

**Consent Check**
: A structured point in a ConsensusUX workflow at which members are explicitly asked whether they consent to proceeding. Unlike passive approval (where silence counts as agreement), a consent check requires active response. Consent checks operationalize the [Informed Consent](design-principles#6-informed-consent) design principle.

**Context Collapse**
: The flattening of distinct social contexts into a single audience, such that communication intended for one group becomes visible to all groups. A message appropriate in a trusted organizing circle may be harmful if visible to hostile actors. ConsensusUX addresses context collapse through [Identity Segmentation](#identity-segmentation) and group-scoped communication design.

**Decolonial Design**
: A design practice and framework (Smith et al., 2024) that interrogates and resists the colonial assumptions embedded in dominant design culture. Operates at three levels: decolonising IN design (recognizing frames of reference), decolonising BY design (collaboration as catalyst, foregrounding alternative epistemologies), and decolonising THROUGH design (mobilizing cracks; moving beyond research to support autonomous action). See: [Academic Foundation — Decolonial Design](academic-foundation#decolonial-design-smith-et-al-2024).

**Degrees of Resistance**
: A graduated scale of opposition available to members during consent checks and proposal processes. Rather than a binary yes/no vote, degrees of resistance allow members to express levels of concern — from mild reservations to outright blocking — providing nuance that binary voting obscures. This enables proposers to understand the character of opposition and modify proposals accordingly.

**Design Justice**
: A framework and movement (associated with Sasha Costanza-Chock and the Design Justice Network) that centers the concerns of communities most affected by design decisions and challenges the reproduction of structural inequalities through design practice. Design justice asks who benefits, who is harmed, and who is consulted — and insists that those who experience the most harm should have the most power to shape solutions.

**Externalizing Cognitive Facilities**
: One of three core platform functions identified by Swann (2021). Refers to the platform's role in handling memory, record-keeping, and coordination tasks that would otherwise burden individual participants and consume cognitive resources needed for deliberation. When the platform remembers meeting decisions, tracks vouch chains, and surfaces relevant history, participants can focus on governance rather than administration.

**Extrinsic Coloring**
: Color used in the UI to convey relational or contextual information — how something relates to the current user, to a current process, or to a temporal state. Contrasted with [Intrinsic Coloring](#intrinsic-coloring). Extrinsic color changes depending on context; the same object may appear differently to different users or in different states. Part of the [Complexity Reduction](design-principles#7-complexity-reduction) principle.

**Federation**
: A model of inter-group coordination in which distinct, self-governing groups maintain autonomous decision-making while establishing voluntary agreements for cooperation, resource sharing, or joint action. In ConsensusUX, federation allows groups to connect without merging and without any group subordinating itself to another. Federalism is a core concept in anarchist organizing tradition.

**Full Member**
: A member of a ConsensusUX group who has completed the vouching process and holds full participation rights: access to all group communications, participation in consent checks, and the right to vouch for prospective members. Contrasted with [Probationary Member](#probationary-member) and [Prospective Member](#prospective-member).

**Functionality Autonomy**
: The principle that groups retain the right to enable or disable platform features according to their own consensus, without permission from a platform administrator. No feature is mandated; no feature is permanently unavailable. Functionality autonomy is an expression of the [No Admin Privileges](design-principles#1-no-admin-privileges) principle at the feature level.

**Green Score (green_score)**
: A trust metric associated with a member within a group, representing positive signals — vouches received, successful participation, constructive engagement. The green score is one component of a member's overall trust standing. Score calculations are transparent and auditable, in keeping with the [Transparency of Process](design-principles#9-transparency-of-process) principle.

**Identity Triangulation**
: The process of combining partial identity information from multiple sources or contexts to build a more complete profile of a person than any single source would reveal. For example, knowing a person's username in Group A and their username in Group B, plus their posting patterns in both, may allow an observer to link the two identities even if the usernames differ. ConsensusUX prevents identity triangulation through [Identity Segmentation](design-principles#5-identity-segmentation).

**Input Chat**
: A communication channel in ConsensusUX oriented toward receiving information — structured intake of proposals, concerns, or reports from members. Input chats are designed for inbound communication flows, where information is being collected for deliberation or decision-making. Contrasted with [Output Chat](#output-chat).

**Intrinsic Coloring**
: Color used in the UI to convey properties inherent to an object regardless of context — a type category, a status that does not change with user or state. Contrasted with [Extrinsic Coloring](#extrinsic-coloring). Intrinsic color is consistent: the same object always appears the same color regardless of who is viewing it or what process is active. Part of the [Complexity Reduction](design-principles#7-complexity-reduction) principle.

**Motifs / Touchpoints**
: Recurring visual or interaction patterns that provide consistent navigational anchors across the platform. Motifs are optional touchpoints — available for users who find them useful, but not required for basic navigation. Their optionality is part of the [Complexity Reduction](design-principles#7-complexity-reduction) principle, which aims to reduce cognitive overhead for groups that do not need them while keeping depth available for those that do.

**Output Chat**
: A communication channel in ConsensusUX oriented toward broadcasting information — announcements, decisions, or outputs from deliberative processes. Output chats carry information from the group to its members, as distinct from [Input Chat](#input-chat), which carries information toward deliberation.

**Prefiguration**
: The principle, central to anarchist and decolonial political traditions, that the means used to pursue a goal must embody the values of that goal. You cannot reach a horizontal, egalitarian future through hierarchical, extractive methods. For ConsensusUX, prefiguration means that the design process itself must be participatory and non-extractive, and that the platform's architecture must instantiate — not merely describe — horizontal relationships.

**Probationary Member**
: A member of a ConsensusUX group who has been admitted but has not yet achieved Full Member status. Probationary members have limited participation rights — they can communicate but may not vouch for prospective members and may have restricted access to certain group functions. The probationary period allows the group to establish trust before extending full rights.

**Prospective Member**
: A person seeking to join a ConsensusUX group who has not yet been admitted. Prospective members are introduced through the [Vouching](#vouching) process. They do not have access to group communications until admitted.

**The Public Sphere**
: Habermas's concept of a discursive space situated between private life and public authority, in which citizens deliberate to form collective opinion and hold power accountable. Singh (2023) defines it as "a network for communicating information and points of view." The public sphere historically existed in physical institutions (coffeehouses, salons, clubs); ConsensusUX is an experiment in whether digital infrastructure can serve this mediating function. See: [Academic Foundation — The Public Sphere](academic-foundation#the-public-sphere-habermas).

**Red Score (red_score)**
: A trust metric associated with a member within a group, representing negative signals — disputes initiated against the member, vouches withdrawn, blocks received. The red score is one component of a member's overall trust standing and is used in combination with the [Green Score](#green-score-green_score). Both scores are transparent and auditable.

**Requisite Variety**
: A principle from cybernetics (Ashby's Law) stating that a control system must have at least as much variety (range of possible states) as the system it is regulating. In platform design, requisite variety means that the platform must be capable of accommodating the full range of governance needs that groups will have — a platform with too few options cannot regulate the complexity of real group life. Swann (2021) applies this principle to radical platform design.

**Safe Defaults**
: The principle that when a group has not yet reached consensus on a setting, the platform applies the most conservative available option — the one that minimizes information exposure and access. Safe defaults ensure that inaction protects rather than exposes. See: [Design Principles — Safe Defaults](design-principles#4-safe-defaults).

**Soft Defaults**
: Settings that apply by default but can be changed by group consensus, as distinct from hard-coded structural properties. Soft defaults represent the platform's best guess at what most groups will want most of the time, but groups retain [Functionality Autonomy](#functionality-autonomy) to change them. Contrasted with [Safe Defaults](#safe-defaults), which specifically govern information exposure in the absence of consensus.

**Soft Lock Dispute**
: A dispute mechanism in which a conflict between two members triggers a temporary constraint affecting both parties symmetrically. Neither party can take certain actions against the other — or in some configurations, within the group — until the dispute is resolved through group process. The symmetrical cost ensures that the mechanism cannot be used as a cost-free attack. See: [Design Principles — Personally Costly Action](design-principles#8-personally-costly-action).

**Sortition**
: A method of selection by random lot, used historically in Athenian democracy and proposed in contemporary democratic theory (including Weyl, Tang and Plurality Community, 2023) as an alternative to election. Sortition distributes participation roles randomly rather than through competition, reducing the influence of wealth, charisma, and political machines. ConsensusUX explores sortition as a mechanism for distributing roles within groups.

**Vouching**
: The process by which existing members of a ConsensusUX group introduce and attest to prospective members. Vouching is not merely approval — vouchers must provide reasoning, staking their own reputation on the introduction. Vouch chains are auditable: the group can trace who vouched for whom and on what basis. Vouching operationalizes [Informed Consent](design-principles#6-informed-consent) at the point of entry and distributes trust-building responsibility across the membership rather than centralizing it in an admin.
